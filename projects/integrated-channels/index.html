01 · Product Context

The problem was bigger than building 12 applications

Banks already operated within complex technology environments. The product therefore had to support domain-specific workflows while remaining configurable, integration-ready, migration-aware, controlled and implementable at client sites.

The product challenge

One platform had to serve very different banking jobs.

Lending, AML, cards, reconciliation, treasury and audit do not share the same users or workflows. The product challenge was to find the reusable platform patterns without flattening the domain complexity.

  • Existing bank applications meant coexistence and integration mattered from day one.
  • Client-specific workflows required configuration rather than a rigid one-size-fits-all product.
  • Regulated processes required roles, validations, approvals, evidence, traceability and reporting.
  • Legacy data required field mapping, transformation, validation and post-migration checks.
  • Deployment success required training, documentation and L1/L2 support readiness.
Product thesis

Standardise the platform. Configure the institution. Integrate the ecosystem.

The suite was approached as a common product foundation with modular domain applications—not as 12 disconnected projects.

StandardiseRoles, controls, workflow patterns, reporting conventions.
ConfigureInstitution-specific business rules and journeys.
IntegrateBank systems, payment rails and external services.
AdoptMigration, deployment, training and support.
02 · Product Strategy

From application list to a reusable banking product ecosystem

The strategy connected platform architecture, domain product design, implementation realities and client adoption. Each decision was intended to reduce repeated effort while preserving the flexibility banks needed.

01

Common platform foundation

Define reusable roles, permissions, workflow states, validations, reporting patterns and auditability before multiplying application complexity.

02

Configurable institution model

Separate reusable product behaviour from bank-specific configuration so workflows can adapt without turning every implementation into a new product.

03

Integration-first product design

Identify systems of record, API contracts, authentication, failures, retries, reconciliation and hand-offs while journeys are being designed.

04

Controls by design

Translate business and regulatory requirements into roles, maker-checker patterns, approval states, validations, evidence and reports.

05

Migration-aware product design

Treat existing data as part of the product problem: assess, map, transform, validate, migrate and verify after migration.

06

Two-phase roadmap

Develop the first six applications during the initial six months, then select and build the remaining six using the foundation and learnings from phase one.

07

Feedback-led refinement

Use progressive product demos, bank feedback, QA and UAT to refine workflows, reports, exceptions and implementation readiness.

08

Adoption as product outcome

Carry product ownership through manuals, training, L1/L2 enablement, deployment readiness, production feedback and enhancement backlog.

01

Platform thinking creates leverage

Reusable roles, workflows, controls and reporting patterns can accelerate multiple domain products while preserving configurability.

02

Integration is part of the UX

Timeouts, invalid data, duplicates, retries, failure ownership and recovery paths must be designed as product behaviour.

03

Controls are product features

In regulated workflows, who acted, under which role, with what evidence and what output is central to the product.

04

Enterprise UX is clarity

Users need to see current state, owner, next action, exception reason and downstream effect without ambiguity.

05

Implementation belongs in product

A feature is not truly successful if migration, integration, deployment, training or support prevents adoption.

06

Senior PM impact is cross-system

End-to-end ownership connects strategy, technology, teams, client constraints, quality, release and measurable outcomes.
